You are given Bertrand's Box Paradox:
There are three identical boxes:
- Box 1 contains 2 gold coins.
- Box 2 contains 2 silver coins.
- Box 3 contains 1 gold coin and 1 silver coin.

You choose a box uniformly at random, and from that chosen box, you draw one coin blindly at random. The drawn coin turns out to be GOLD.

Question:
What is the exact conditional probability that the remaining coin in the chosen box is also GOLD?
Requirements:
1. Explain why the naive intuitive answer of 1/2 is incorrect.
2. Provide a rigorous formal proof using Bayes' Theorem, clearly stating the priors P(Box_i), likelihoods P(Gold | Box_i), and the total probability P(Gold).
3. Verify the result using sample space counting of individual coin faces (6 total coin faces).
