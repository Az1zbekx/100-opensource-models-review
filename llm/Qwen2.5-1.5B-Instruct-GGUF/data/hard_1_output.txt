### 1. Why the naive intuitive answer of 1/2 is incorrect

The naive intuitive answer of 1/2 is incorrect because it assumes that the probability of choosing each box is equal, which is not true. The probability of choosing each box is not 1/3, but rather the probability of choosing each box is proportional to the number of gold coins in that box.

### 2. Rigorous formal proof using Bayes' Theorem

We will use Bayes' Theorem to find the exact conditional probability that the remaining coin in the chosen box is also GOLD.

Let's define the following:
- \( B_1 \): Box 1 (2 gold coins)
- \( B_2 \): Box 2 (2 silver coins)
- \( B_3 \): Box 3 (1 gold coin and 1 silver coin)

We are given that we have drawn a gold coin from one of these boxes. We want to find the probability that the remaining coin in the chosen box is also gold.

#### Step 1: Define the events
- \( A \): The event that the drawn coin is gold.
- \( B_i \): The event that the chosen box is \( B_i \).

#### Step 2: Write down the probabilities
- The probability of choosing each box is proportional to the number of gold coins in that box:
  \[
  P(B_1) = \frac{2}{2+2+1} = \frac{2}{5}
  \]
  \[
  P(B_2) = \frac{2}{2+2+1} = \frac{2}{5}
  \]
  \[
  P(B_3) = \frac{1}{2+2+1} = \frac{1}{5}
  \]

- The probability of drawing a gold coin from each box is:
  \[
  P(A|B_1) = \frac{2}{2+2} = \frac{1}{2}
  \]
  \[
  P(A|B_2) = \frac{2}{2+2} = \frac{1}{2}
  \]
  \[
  P(A|B_3) = \frac{1}{1+1} = \frac{1}{2}
  \]

#### Step 3: Apply Bayes' Theorem
Bayes' Theorem states:
\[
P(B_i|A) = \frac{P(A|B_i) \cdot P(B_i)}{P(A)}
\]

First, we need to find \( P(A) \), the total probability of drawing a gold coin:
\[
P(A) = P(A|B_1) \cdot P(B_1) + P(A|B_2) \cdot P(B_2) + P(A|B_3) \cdot P(B_3)
\]
\[
P(A) = \left(\frac{1}{2} \cdot \frac{2}{5}\right) + \left(\frac{1}{2} \cdot \frac{2}{5}\right) + \left(\frac{1}{2} \cdot \frac{1}{5}\right)
\]
\[
P(A) = \frac{1}{5} + \frac{1}{5} + \frac{1}{10} = \frac{2}{10} + \frac{2}{10} + \frac{1}{10} = \frac{5}{10} = \frac{1}{2}
\]

Now, we can find \( P(B_i|A) \) for each box:
\[
P(B_1|A) = \frac{P(A|B_1) \cdot P(B_1)}{P(A)} = \frac{\frac{1}{2} \cdot \frac{2}{5}}{\frac{1}{2}} = \frac{\frac{1}{5}}{\frac{1}{2}} = \frac{2}{5}
\]
\[
P(B_2|A) = \frac{P(A|B_2) \cdot P(B_2)}{P(A)} = \frac{\frac{1}{2} \cdot \frac{2}{5}}{\frac{1}{2}} = \frac{\frac{1}{5}}{\frac{1}{2}} = \frac{2}{5}
\]
\[
P(B_3|A) = \frac{P(A|B_3) \cdot P(B_3)}{P(A)} = \frac{\frac{1}{2} \cdot \frac{1}{5}}{\frac{1}{2}} = \frac{\frac{1}{10}}{\frac{1}{2}} = \frac{1}{5}
\]
